\begin{abstract}
The automation of agricultural surveying is a critical step toward maximizing global harvest yields and ensuring sustainable food production. Historically, crop density and yield forecasting have relied heavily on manual inspection, which introduces significant human error and limits scalability. To address these bottlenecks, we introduce CropSense, an integrated computer vision framework optimized for the rapid and precise detection of wheat heads in natural field environments. By leveraging a heavily customized YOLOv8 small (YOLOv8s) model trained on the expansive Global Wheat Detection dataset, our methodology provides robust bounding-box localization that naturally adapts to varying light conditions, overlapping canopies, and dense growth patterns. Furthermore, we translate raw bounding-box detections into meaningful agricultural metrics by applying spatial uniformity calculations and localized density estimations. The experimental evaluation confirms that CropSense achieves competitive mean Average Precision (mAP) while effectively mitigating edge cases such as extreme shadowing. To ensure practical usability, the framework features a streamlined, automated reporting pipeline that instantly transforms complex machine learning outputs into digestible PDF analytics, thereby offering an end-to-end tool for modern agronomic decision-making.
\end{abstract}
